# DockProof — every claim dollar, traced

[2:44 narrated demo](https://youtu.be/PxcxiehxLzY) · [Try the scan case](https://blucca.github.io/dockproof/?case=scan-example)

A damaged shipment leaves a small shipping team with scattered records and a costly question: how much should we claim? This worksheet uses all six hundred pounds and asks for three thousand dollars. DockProof traces the amount for one damaged crate, B4.

I bring eight synthetic evidence files to the desk: a mixed PDF, an invoice, warehouse records, a printed label, and a damage photo. Local English OCR reads the scanned delivery page and label. Original files stay in the browser, alongside their extracted text.

Now, Extract with Nemotron. This is a real NVIDIA Nemotron Super request on Nebius Token Factory. The model reads the document text and proposes typed values with exact source excerpts. DockProof locates those excerpts for review, including the scanned delivery date.

The reviewer checks the source before accepting each selected fact. The original worksheet carries six hundred pounds for the whole shipment. B4's own record supports one hundred fifty pounds. Both sources stay available. The affected piece, the original worksheet, and the chosen amount each keep their own evidence.

The calculation follows that evidence. Two thousand dollars in goods, less a hundred-dollar discount and one hundred seventy-five dollars in retained salvage, gives seventeen hundred twenty-five dollars in loss. The selected class-seventy terms give a seven-hundred-fifty-dollar reference limit and proposed demand.

After reviewing the sources and terms, I approve this evidence snapshot. DockProof prepares the claim letter, valuation, rule references, and all eight original files with their fingerprints. The shipper can download the packet and use the carrier's filing process. Carrier receipt is a separate recorded event.
